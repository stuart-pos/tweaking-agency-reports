One brand at a time

Midi

The insured buyer who wants a video visit, FDA-approved medication, and a pharmacy copay.

Insurance-covered virtual practice in all 50 states. Revenue is the visit, not a medication subscription. No membership. Labs and prescriptions go to outside labs and local pharmacies. Self-pay is $250 first visit, $150 follow-up.

Prices on the site

  • Self-pay $250 first visit / $150 follow-up
  • Compounded GLP-1 from $164 per 4 weeks, shipping included
  • Microdosing advertised from $34/week
  • Brand GLP-1 sent to the patient’s pharmacy

Why they win

  • Specialist care, not a cash medication store
  • Midlife women’s health clinicians
  • Local pharmacy and FDA-approved HRT
  • 25k+ patients/week, 45M women covered by insurance, $250M+ raised, valued above $1B (Feb 2026)

Praise. Felt heard after being dismissed. Fast HRT, insurance, pharmacy convenience.

Where it breaks. Provider quality varies. Billing surprises. Hard to reschedule. Some visits feel scripted.

Search, Meta, SEO, and employer/payer deals. Semrush: 692K organic visits and 6.51K paid-search visits in Aug 2026. Amy Schumer national campaign in 2026 across TV, radio, out-of-home, and social.

Winona pricing, and why it says “from”

Every hormone product on Winona’s price page starts with “from.” The add-ons that are not hormone therapy show one flat price. The “from” number is the cheapest version of that product. What a patient actually pays is set after the doctor writes the prescription, and Winona shows that amount before payment. The five things that move the price

  1. Dose. The doctor picks the strength from the intake and can raise it at the 10-week check-in. Winona does not publish a price for each strength, so “from” covers the cheapest version and outside reviewers report that higher strengths cost more. The patch is the clearest case: five strengths behind one “from $149.”
  2. Form of the same hormone. Estradiol is $54 as a tablet, $89 as a cream, and $149 as a patch. The doctor and patient pick the form, and the form sets the price band.
  3. Number of products. Women with a uterus who take systemic estrogen also need progesterone to protect the uterine lining. Two products means two prices. The exception is the $89 combo cream, which has both hormones.
  4. 30-day or 90-day supply. Winona says a 90-day supply carries “a small cost saving” but does not say how much. Patients choose during onboarding.
  5. Which pharmacy fills it. Winona’s own pharmacy includes care and free shipping. Sending the prescription to an outside pharmacy adds a $50 monthly platform fee, and the patient pays that pharmacy for the medication.
“Per month” is billed every 28 days. Winona processes a 30-day supply every 28 days and a 90-day supply every 84 days. That is 13 charges a year, not 12. The $89 cream is $1,157 a year, not $1,068.

What this means for Noonie. A woman with a uterus pays Winona $89–$93 every 28 days on the cheapest routes. Noonie’s flat $99/month only wins if it covers both hormones, does not rise with dose, and bills monthly rather than every 28 days. If that is true, “one price, any dose, billed monthly” is a claim Winona cannot make. Confirm all three with Noonie before using it in ads.

Where Noonie can fit

A usable position. Clear hormone care for women who are done being dismissed: a real clinician visit, a written plan, a transparent total, and a person they can reach. The narrower wedge is symptom-led HRT with testosterone and no routine panel, between Winona’s no-lab simplicity and Joi’s lab-first model. Keep GLP-1 off the homepage pitch. Winona does not prescribe testosterone or GLP-1. That opening only works after Noonie says who it is for and what $99 includes.

The current site is fast and shows $99/month, FSA/HSA, free shipping, and no required hormone testing. It does not yet give a buyer a reason to pick Noonie over brands with more proof and a clearer care model.

Treatments to add

Checked on noonie.com on October 13, 2026. The live catalog is GLP-1 injections, estradiol cream, oral progesterone, testosterone cream, and cortisol support. Against Winona’s menopause catalog, add these three. Do not copy Winona’s face cream, arousal cream, hair serum, or sleep product.

  1. Vaginal estradiol. The current estradiol cream page does not say whether the cream is a systemic body cream or a local vaginal treatment. Vaginal dryness, painful sex, and urinary symptoms are a separate purchase, and The Menopause Society treats local low-dose vaginal estrogen as an effective option for those symptoms.
  2. Estradiol patch. This is the standard systemic format patients ask for when they do not want a compounded cream. Prefer an FDA-approved patch filled through a pharmacy.
  3. A combined plan: estradiol patch or estradiol cream, plus oral progesterone. Noonie already lists the cream and oral progesterone as separate $99 products. Women with a uterus who use systemic estrogen generally also need a progestogen. Sell that as one plan with one price, not as two full-price products.

How Winona prices these three

Winona does not publish a price for each strength. The doctor sets the dose, Winona shows the resulting price before payment, and the “from” number is only the cheapest version. A 90-day supply has a small saving Winona does not quantify. Sending the prescription to an outside pharmacy adds a $50 monthly platform fee, and the patient then pays that pharmacy for the drug. Systemic products are billed every 28 days, which is 13 charges a year.
