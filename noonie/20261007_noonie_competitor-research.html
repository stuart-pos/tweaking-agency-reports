One brand at a time

Midi

The insured buyer who wants a video visit, FDA-approved medication, and a pharmacy copay.

Insurance-covered virtual practice in all 50 states. Revenue is the visit, not a medication subscription. No membership. Labs and prescriptions go to outside labs and local pharmacies. Self-pay is $250 first visit, $150 follow-up.

Prices on the site

  • Self-pay $250 first visit / $150 follow-up
  • Compounded GLP-1 from $164 per 4 weeks, shipping included
  • Microdosing advertised from $34/week
  • Brand GLP-1 sent to the patient’s pharmacy

Why they win

  • Specialist care, not a cash medication store
  • Midlife women’s health clinicians
  • Local pharmacy and FDA-approved HRT
  • 25k+ patients/week, 45M women covered by insurance, $250M+ raised, valued above $1B (Feb 2026)

Praise. Felt heard after being dismissed. Fast HRT, insurance, pharmacy convenience.

Where it breaks. Provider quality varies. Billing surprises. Hard to reschedule. Some visits feel scripted.

Search, Meta, SEO, and employer/payer deals. Semrush: 692K organic visits and 6.51K paid-search visits in Aug 2026. Amy Schumer national campaign in 2026 across TV, radio, out-of-home, and social.

Winona

The cash buyer who wants no labs, no video visit, one price, and delivery.

Cash-pay menopause subscription for women 35–59. Care, messaging, and shipping are inside the medication price. Owns two 503A pharmacies. Does not prescribe testosterone, GLP-1, or pellets.

Prices on the site

  • Progesterone from $39/mo
  • Estrogen tablets from $54/mo
  • Estrogen cream, combo cream, vaginal cream from $89/mo
  • Estrogen patch from $149/mo
  • DHEA $27 / 3 months

Why they win

  • Questionnaire, review, ship. No required labs.
  • Care is included in the drug price
  • Owned pharmacy and compounded formulas
  • 23K+ Facebook group and a large review base

Praise. Fast start, responsive messages, symptom relief, no separate doctor bill.

Where it breaks. Compounded-drug trust. Cash can cost more than insured retail. Cancellation, renewals, and lost shipments. Care can feel transactional.

Meta creates demand, Google captures it, email/SMS recovers checkout. Agency reports $1.5M/month across Meta, Google, Taboola, and Pinterest at 3.2x ROAS. About 2,887 active Meta ads. Semrush: 5.07K paid-search visits and 29.6K organic visits in Aug 2026.

Joi

The buyer whose doctor would not order or read a broad hormone panel.

Diagnostics-first cash platform. Labs, then a consult, then a care plan. Makes money on labs, a $50/month care fee billed quarterly, medication, supplements, and GLP-1. Shares the backend with Blokes. Medications are on top of the care fee.

Prices on the site

  • Care $50/mo, billed quarterly
  • Promo panels Oct 7: 56 markers $74, 71 markers $140, 110 markers $245. Recheck at checkout.
  • Testosterone $59 injection or $69 cream
  • Estrogen $49 capsule, $69 cream, $129 patch
  • Progesterone $54 capsule or $69 cream

Why they win

  • Labs first, then a plan
  • Live clinician visits, follow-up labs, coaching
  • Wide catalog raises lifetime value

Praise. Thorough panels. Felt heard. Access to therapies local care would not offer.

Where it breaks. Total cost gets messy. Delays, support turnover, pharmacy confusion, surprise fees, aggressive cross-sell. FDA warning letter in June 2026 on compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide claims.

Meta, Google, affiliates, podcasts, CTV. Public Google footprint is much smaller than Midi or Winona and sits under the Blokes advertiser. Creator spend is concentrated, not broad.

Blokes

Men buying performance and a bundled plan, not a pile of separate bills.

Men’s version of Joi: TRT, performance, longevity, weight. Care, labs, coaching, and community are inside the TRT plan. Add-ons raise the order.

Prices on the site

  • 3 months: $149/mo, $447 upfront
  • 6 months: $129/mo, $774 upfront
  • 12 months: $99/mo, $1,188 upfront
  • Hair and ED add-ons advertised at $1/mo for active members

Why they win

  • One TRT price instead of separate lab, care, and drug bills
  • Energy, muscle, libido, mood, sleep
  • Athlete and podcast endorsements

Praise. Bundled plan and a long consult. Ratings are mixed with Joi, so they are less diagnostic.

Where it breaks. Price and long commitments. Support and fulfillment complaints.

Podcasts, creators, affiliates, CTV. 120 tracked YouTube videos, 106 of them on one show. Not a search leader.

Where Noonie can fit

A usable position. Clear hormone care for women who are done being dismissed: a real clinician visit, a written plan, a transparent total, and a person they can reach. The narrower wedge is symptom-led HRT with testosterone and no routine panel, between Winona’s no-lab simplicity and Joi’s lab-first model. Keep GLP-1 off the homepage pitch. Winona does not prescribe testosterone or GLP-1. That opening only works after Noonie says who it is for and what $99 includes.

The current site is fast and shows $99/month, FSA/HSA, free shipping, and no required hormone testing. It does not yet give a buyer a reason to pick Noonie over brands with more proof and a clearer care model.

Evidence limits

  • Prices come from official pages on October 7, 2026. Promo prices should be rechecked at checkout.
  • Semrush visits and social counts are directional, not invoices.
  • Google Ads Transparency shows creatives, not spend.
  • Winona’s $1.5M/month and 3.2x ROAS are agency-reported across channels, not Google-only, and not audited.
  • Reviews describe failure modes. They are not a measure of typical clinical quality.
