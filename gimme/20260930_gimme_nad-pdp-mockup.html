What is NAD+?

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme naturally found in every cell in the body. It plays an important role in cellular energy production and metabolic processes.

Some medical providers may recommend NAD+ as part of a wellness or metabolic support plan.

What is NAD+ treatment used for?

Because NAD+ is involved in cellular energy and metabolic pathways, some providers use it in care plans that focus on areas such as metabolic health, recovery, and overall wellness support.

Your physician will determine whether NAD+ is appropriate based on your medical history and goals from our questionnaire.

How does NAD+ work in the body?

NAD+ supports several biological processes in the body, including cellular energy production and enzyme activity involved in metabolism and cellular repair.

Levels of NAD+ can naturally change with age and other factors. A medical provider may evaluate whether NAD+ supplementation could be appropriate as part of a supervised care plan.

What are the potential side effects?

Because NAD+ injections are compounded, complete information about the frequency of side effects is limited. Possible reactions can include injection-site irritation, flushing, nausea, headache, or fatigue. Contact your provider if symptoms persist or worsen. Seek urgent medical care for severe chills, shaking, repeated vomiting, trouble breathing, fainting, or other intense or unexpected symptoms. This is not a complete list of possible side effects.

What is NAD+?New

Every cell runs on NAD+. You make less with age.

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every cell. It helps turn the food you eat into cellular energy and powers the enzymes that repair DNA.1,2

Studies in healthy adults show NAD+ levels fall as we age.2,3 Lower NAD+ is linked to age-related changes in metabolism, muscle, and brain health.1,4

Why an injection, not a pill

Most NAD+ supplements are precursors like NR or NMN that your body has to convert, and oral forms are processed by your gut and liver first.4,5 An injection delivers NAD+ itself.

The research behind this page

Every claim above links to a peer-reviewed source.

  1. Review of how NAD+ drives cellular energy metabolism and DNA repair, and how NAD+ imbalance contributes to aging, metabolic disease, and neurodegeneration. Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy, 2020
  2. Study of skin tissue from 49 people aged 0 to 77. NAD+ levels declined with age in both men and women, while DNA damage increased. PLoS One, 2012
  3. Study of plasma from healthy adults aged 20 to 87 showing a significant decline in NAD+ with age. Median NAD+ was about 55 nmol/L at ages 20–40, 37 at 41–60, and 7 at 60+ (29 adults, read from Figure 2). Rejuvenation Research, 2019
  4. Review of NAD+ precursors in age-related disease. Precursors raise NAD+ levels in human studies, but clinical benefits have been limited so far, and oral forms interact with gut bacteria. Biochemical and Biophysical Research Communications, 2024
  5. Review of NAD+ and mitochondria in aging. Oral precursors are metabolized by the liver and gut, while direct NAD+ has raised cellular NAD+ in small trials in healthy and older adults, with signs of improved cognitive function. npj Metabolic Health and Disease, 2025
  6. First human study tracking directly administered NAD+. Infused NAD+ was rapidly and completely removed from the blood for the first 2 hours as the body took it up and metabolized it. Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience, 2019
  7. Double-blind crossover study in 12 older men given one dose of nicotinamide riboside, an oral NAD+ booster. Fatigue index improved 15% and isometric strength 8%, with lower oxidative stress. European Journal of Nutrition, 2019
