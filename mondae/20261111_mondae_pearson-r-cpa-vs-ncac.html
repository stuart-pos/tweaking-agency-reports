Pearson r for CPA and nCAC

Mondae · Nov 11, 2026 · Ghost Google account vs ghost nCAC, and how we will use the same score as the mix changes.

Pearson r

Pearson r is a score from −1 to +1 for how closely two series move together in size. +1 means they rise and fall by matching amounts. 0 means no straight-line relationship. It is not a count of how often they point the same way, and it is not a measure of how close the two levels are. A $3 move and a $60 move do not count the same.

R-squared is r squared. It runs from 0 to 1 and drops the direction. At r = 0.92, R-squared is about 0.85: about 85% of the day-to-day swing in one number lined up with the swing in the other.

How we want to use it

We want r, read next to the average gap, as the check on whether a platform CPA is still a usable read of nCAC. The level of either number can move because of a lull, a budget cut, or a CPC jump. r ignores that average. It only asks whether the days with a higher reported CPA were also the days with a higher nCAC, and by a similar amount.

Three numbers, used together:

  • r — did the size of the moves line up.
  • Direction — how many days both rose or both fell. A plain-language read. A small move counts the same as a large one.
  • Gap — how far the platform CPA sits from nCAC. This is the accuracy. r does not contain it. A platform can run $35 low every day and still have r = 0.92.

From today through November 17

Nov 11–17, seven days. We score the same relationship once Meta spend is in Ghost. We are not matching a 14-day window to a 14-day window.

If both CPA and nCAC move from $230 to $350, the averages changed and the question did not. On the days reported CPA was higher, was nCAC higher with it? If r stays near 0.92, Meta’s CPA is still a usable read of Ghost’s cost. If r falls, Meta can look cheap in-platform and still be a bad read of nCAC.

Seven days is a directional check, noisier than 0.92. We read a clear hold or a clear drop, and we put Meta’s share of Ghost spend next to r. A larger Meta share pushes r up on its own, because Meta’s CPA is then part of nCAC.

Why the score is valuable

A $250 nCAC gate tells us whether the week was cheap enough to keep spending. It does not tell us whether the number in the ad platform still describes that cost. r does. It stays comparable when the auction, the budget, or the season moves both series up or down together.

The Ghost result is the proof that the score can be high while the platform number is still biased. r = 0.92 and Google ran about $35 low. We can use Google’s CPA as a day-to-day guide for Ghost, and we still add about $35 if we want the nCAC. If a new mix keeps a high r and a stable gap, we can manage off the platform CPA. If r falls, we stop treating that CPA as the cost of a customer.

How we plan to use it

  1. Keep Oct 22–Nov 4 as the Google-only baseline: r = 0.92, about a $35 gap, last-click nCAC.
  2. Score Nov 11–17 the same way, with Meta’s share of Ghost spend written next to r.
  3. Hold the current mix (Meta + Shopping + Brand) only while nCAC is under $250.
  4. Run Meta + Brand for a short window and score r and the gap again.
  5. A high r with a stable gap means the platform CPA is a fair stand-in. A lower r means the mix broke that stand-in, even if the in-platform CPA looks better.

Definitions

  • nCAC. Customers from the g. subdomain divided by Ghost spend. Ghost spend is Mondae New Google (987-594-0392) plus the Ghost Meta account. The order count in Slack is last-click. The $250 gate uses this number.
  • Attribution. Every purchase that comes through the g. subdomain, including direct and organic.
  • Google CPA. Mondae New’s platform-reported Purchase CPA. The $234 figure is total spend divided by total purchases for Oct 22–Nov 4.
  • Meta CPA. The Ghost Meta account’s platform-reported cost per purchase.
  • Pearson r. Co-movement in size, from −1 to +1. R-squared is r squared and does not show direction.
